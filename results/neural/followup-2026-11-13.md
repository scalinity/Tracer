# Follow-up on the open caveats, November 13

Synthetic data throughout: laboratory weeks designed by language models and simulated by this project's code, with no clinical review.

Nothing here touched `test`. No test look was taken, the test lock is unchanged, and no OpenAI money was spent.

## 1. The negative control's leakage flag: a false alarm caused by the training population

The A6 rule flags leakage when TracerNet leads the local analytic refit by 5 minutes or more on the v1-world control. It did: +10 minutes median warning (95% interval 5 to 20). The earlier explanation, that the local refit's history was too small, is wrong. Three checks (`python -m src.pipeline.control_audit`, results in `results/neural/control_audit/summary.json`):

| Check | Median lead over local refit | Capped mean lead | Flagged |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analytic model fit on 40 fresh v1-world weeks | 0 (-5 to 5) | -1.09 (-1.49 to -0.72) | No |
| Same analytic model fit on a 10% sample of the v2 train weeks | +10 (10 to 20) | +2.78 (2.08 to 3.44) | Yes |
| TracerNet | +10 (5 to 20) | +1.27 | Yes |

- A big local history does not beat the local refit, so history size is not the cause.
- A model that reads no context at all, fit on the same v2 weeks TracerNet trained on, gets the same 10-minute lead. It cannot be leaking context, so the lead comes from the v2 population's risk shape.
- TracerNet reads nothing after a tick: on 4 control weeks cut at 12 points, deleting every later scan changed 0 of 35,499 earlier scores (`invariance.json`, largest change 0.0).
- Fitting TracerNet's output to the control's own history (section 2) removes the lead: -5 minutes against the local refit, not flagged.

Verdict: leakage is not supported. A6 compares a v2-trained model with a v1-world fit, so it measures the population gap. A later control should judge leakage against the analytic model fit on TracerNet's own training weeks, where the committed control already shows no lead (0, interval -5 to 0).

## 2. Calibration on `legacy_v1`: closed with a site map fit on history only

`src/neural/sitecal.py` fits one small map per laboratory on that laboratory's history and never reads its scored weeks:

    logit p' = a + b * logit p + c * log(minutes silent / 30)

The form was chosen on history alone, fitting runs 0 to 15 and checking runs 16 to 23 (`results/neural/sitecal/form_cv.json`):

| Brier on check runs | 30 min | 60 min |
|---|---|---|
| No map | 0.0937 | 0.1401 |
| Two-term map | 0.1021 | 0.1222 |
| Three-term map (chosen) | 0.0949 | 0.1203 |

On the scored `legacy_v1` weeks, with the map fit on runs 0 to 23:

| Brier | 30 min | 60 min |
|---|---|---|
| Analytic model | 0.1093 | 0.1817 |
| TracerNet before | 0.1188 | 0.2155 |
| TracerNet with site map | 0.1122 | 0.1814 |

R3 now holds on `legacy_v1`. One trade-off showed up: alerting on the mapped scores cut the median warning from 55 to 47.5 minutes at 3-2-1 weights. So the map is for the probability a lab sees, and alerts keep TracerNet's own ranking. That split was decided after seeing the scored `legacy_v1` weeks, which are development data. On the control, with only one week of history, the map helped at 60 minutes (0.229 to 0.213) and hurt at 30 (0.110 to 0.118), so it needs several weeks of history to be worth using.

## 3. R1 cannot be met on `test` at 6 alerts per shift

`test` has 6.4 breaches per shift against a budget of 6 alerts, and no method catches more than 39.2%. A miss counts as 0 warning minutes, so every method's median warning is 0 and no lead is possible. The rule as written cannot separate the methods there. This is disclosed, not fixed: the rule stays as pre-registered and R1 stays failed.

For any future look, the proposed secondary measure is the capped mean warning, which spec §8.5 already defines and the evaluation already reports. On the first test look TracerNet led every baseline on it, by about 10 to 18 minutes, with every interval above zero. That number was already visible before this proposal, so it is not a pre-registered result and is not offered as one.

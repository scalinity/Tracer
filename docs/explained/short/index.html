Both of you

Quiz each other.

Isn't it circular to test on data you made?

Partly, yes. It shows the method behaves as designed, not that it works in a real lab. That's why the next step is a real log.

Why not just alert on everything after 30 minutes?

Windows run from 60 minutes for urine to days for a swab, so one limit is too late for some and far too early for others.

What would someone do with an alert?

Go to the place Tracer names and look. Who gets which alert is our assumption; a real lab would set it.

How many false alarms?

Plenty, by design: about a third of specimens get an alert, and most arrive in time. The gain is in timing, not sorting.

Is this machine learning?

A small statistical model: its shape is chosen by hand and its numbers are learned from data. No neural network, and Claude predicts nothing.

How often is it right about where?

At 30 minutes of silence, its first guess is right about 55% of the time and its top three about 88%.

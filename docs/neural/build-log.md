# Build log: v2 scenario data and TracerNet

A running diary of the build that `docs/neural/spec.md` describes: what was done, in what order, what was found, and every call made along the way. Newest entries last. Times are US Eastern. The pull-request record is `docs/ai-teaming-log.md`; this log is the day-by-day detail behind it.

Every number this work reports comes from synthetic data with no clinical review.

**11:42 PM, a secondary analysis, fixed before any real validation or test result exists.** The evaluation driver's smoke check on the 8-scenario development set showed the headline metric collapsing: at 6 alerts per shift, the median warning over all breaches (a miss counting as zero) was 0 for almost every method, because those laboratories have more breaches per shift than the budget has alerts. The pre-registered rule of spec §8.6 stays binding exactly as written and the test set is still scored once. This is decided now, before the real validation set has been scored, so it cannot have been chosen to favor any method.

**1:12 AM to 1:16 AM, the freeze.** The first attempt re-validated the 6,092 accepted scenarios one at a time (each check simulates a full week), which would have taken about half an hour on one core; it was stopped before writing anything and the re-validation now runs in worker processes. Every scenario passed again. 21 near duplicates were left out, leaving 6,071 scenarios in 5,396 families: train 3,668, validation 772, test 782 and out-of-distribution test 849. Coverage holds in every cell, factor level and edge tag. The library is far less spread out than code-random scenarios (a Vendi score of 73 on 2,000 against 1,974), as expected from a model writing plausible laboratories rather than uniform noise.

**The out-of-distribution set.** A family goes to `test_ood` whole if any member is a multi-site system or has a scanner outage. Union-find at 1.5 times the near-duplicate distance chains some dense regions into large families (the largest has 147 scenarios), and each of the six largest holds an out-of-distribution member, so 367 of the 849 `test_ood` scenarios are ordinary weeks that travel with their family. That keeps near-identical weeks out of two splits, which is the point of families; the report will give `test_ood` results on the 482 scenarios that carry the feature as well as on the whole set.

**12:21 AM to 12:41 AM, putting N6 together.** Two agents built store v2, the observed-only feature builder, TracerNet's network and the evaluation driver in separate worktrees; they were merged on one integration branch, where the full suite passed (1,393 tests). Three seams needed fixing: the store did not read a frozen record's ids from its `meta` block, a feature schema read back from disk ordered its series alphabetically (which the network refuses), and nothing produced TracerNet's risk table for the driver, so `evaluate --artifact` now scores a set with the trained artifact first.

**Two slow layers.** The agent building training measured rung B at about 727 samples a second on CPU against 53,000 on the Apple GPU. A profile found two culprits in PyTorch's CPU code: the dilated causal convolution takes a slow path (and slows down with more threads), and the backward pass of a 1x1 convolution loops over the batch. Both are now the same arithmetic written as shifted slices and one `einsum`, with the same parameters and outputs equal to about 1e-6. One process now scores about 25,000 ticks a second on CPU, roughly 13 times the previous four-process rate. That matters because final test scoring runs on CPU to be bit-reproducible.

**12:43 AM to 12:52 AM, an independent review of the evaluation's integrity.** GPT-6.1 Sol (read-only) checked the test lock, truth isolation, label-free knob tuning, the decision rule and validation-only training. Truth isolation held, and the decision rule matched the spec. It found four critical defects: TracerNet could score a test set before the lock was taken; the lock's fingerprint left out inputs that change reported numbers (which risk table, the urgency weights, the training store); store and artifact manifests were trusted without checking the files against them; and knob tuning could return a threshold whose history volume exceeded the budget (47.6 alerts per shift against 6 in its probe). Four warnings followed: R4's matched-volume check ran only when asked, the worker cache key sampled the data, rung selection could keep B or C with its warning check unrun, and the predictor tuned one knob across scenarios.

**12:52 AM to 1:33 AM, the fixes.** An Opus agent fixed all eight, each with a test shown to fail on the old code (18 tests). The lock is taken before any test inference and reused; it covers every input that changes a number; files are verified against their manifests; v2 tuning has a no-alert knob, so a budget is never exceeded; R4's sensitivity runs whenever R4 fails; the cache key is a full digest; selection refuses to finish without its warning check; and the predictor tunes per scenario on history alone. The `legacy_v1` set still reproduces `results/evaluation.json` exactly.

**What the strict budget means.** In a laboratory with more breaches per shift than the budget has alerts, every method whose risk reaches 1 at the window's close (all of them) has no knob that both alerts and stays in budget, so it alerts on nothing there. Before the fix those scenarios ran over budget without anyone seeing it. The evaluation now counts them per method and budget, and the report states the counts beside R1.

**1:24 AM to 1:31 AM, store v2.** 20,616 simulated weeks became `data/store-v2/`: 48.9 million specimens and 228 million scan events in `observed.duckdb` (3.7 GB), and the truth in a separate `truth.duckdb` (7.7 GB). Only the observed file was copied to HiPerGator for training (verified by SHA-256); the truth never leaves this Mac.

**Memory.** A three-minute training benchmark on the real store pushed this Mac to 44 of its 45 GB of swap: the training table and its twelve feature workers held about 20 GB beside everything else the Mac was running. It was stopped. Training runs on HiPerGator, where a job can ask for 100 GB; this Mac keeps the evaluation and the CPU scoring.

**The re-review, and a defect real data found.** Sol's second pass closed five of the eight findings and raised five narrower warnings: a recorded test run could still score a different artifact or store, the lock did not record which risk-table column was read, a separate training store without a manifest had no identity, the predictor applied the v2 no-alert knob to v1 tuning, and harm clipped invalid probabilities instead of refusing them. All five are fixed with tests. The first validation run on the real store then stopped on its own new check: the locally refit analytic model gave a risk that was not a number. A history week with no never-received specimen fits a mixture whose never-received share is 0, so a specimen silent past both of its components gets 0/0. Such a scenario now uses the global fit for its history and scored weeks alike, as spec §8.2 says to do when a refit fails, and the note says why (4 of the 772 validation scenarios).
